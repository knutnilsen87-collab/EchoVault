# MVP-to-Market-Leader Roadmap

## Purpose

This roadmap connects the practical MVP plan to the complete target product. It prevents two failure modes:

1. Building only a thin MVP with no route to dominance.
2. Building a fantasy platform before demand is proven.

## Stage 0 — Strategy lock

**Goal:** Confirm wedge, promise and red lines.

**Must be true before build:**
- Primary wedge is B2B licensing, not consumer streaming.
- First ICP is selected.
- Legal counsel has reviewed launch language or risk posture.
- Team agrees EchoScore is technical QA, not legal validation.
- Team agrees PoE is provenance/process evidence, not absolute proof.

**Output:**
- ICP memo.
- License tier draft.
- Review policy draft.
- Initial risk register.
- MVP scope freeze.

## Stage 1 — 1x MVP: prove the licensing loop

**Goal:** Prove that at least one target buyer will pay for an approved AI/hybrid track with a certificate.

**Build:**
- Artist upload.
- PoE form.
- EchoScore analysis.
- Admin review.
- Buyer catalog.
- Preview.
- Stripe checkout.
- License ledger.
- PDF certificate.
- Signed file delivery.
- Buyer dashboard.
- Basic dispute intake.

**DoD:**
- Paid purchase works end-to-end.
- No duplicate license on webhook retry.
- Certificate includes terms version.
- High-res file is never public.
- Admin can suspend a track.
- Legal disclaimers are approved.
- At least 20–50 real paid purchases or comparable high-intent validation in beta.

## Stage 2 — 10x product-ready: repeatable commercial workflow

**Goal:** Prove the model works repeatedly without founder hand-holding.

**Build:**
- Project folders.
- Saved tracks.
- License upgrades.
- Public/private certificate verification page.
- Review reason taxonomy.
- Reviewer QA workflow.
- Search analytics.
- Artist analytics.
- Improved support/dispute workflow.
- Better catalog merchandising by use case.
- Event tracking and dashboards.

**DoD:**
- Repeat buyers exist.
- Review time is predictable.
- Support questions decrease through better UX.
- Catalog supply is shaped by buyer demand.
- Product can onboard new artists without bespoke calls.
- Operations team can manage disputes with evidence packs.

## Stage 3 — 25x marketplace expansion: deepen liquidity

**Goal:** Expand from one wedge into adjacent buyer segments without losing trust.

**Build:**
- More license templates.
- Agency/team accounts.
- Multi-client project tracking.
- Invoice/receipt management.
- More advanced search and recommendations.
- Curated collections.
- Artist reputation tiers.
- Fraud/risk scoring for review prioritization.
- Payout automation.

**DoD:**
- GMV grows with repeatable acquisition channels.
- Approved catalog grows without quality collapse.
- Buyer demand signals inform artist recruitment.
- Take rate and support cost create sustainable unit economics.
- Dispute rate remains within acceptable guardrails.

## Stage 4 — 50x platform: integrations and API

**Goal:** Become embedded in buyer workflows and partner channels.

**Build:**
- API keys and scopes.
- Search/preview/license/certificate API.
- Partner dashboards.
- Webhooks.
- Video/editor/game-tool integrations.
- Enterprise procurement package.
- SSO/team roles for larger accounts.
- Audit exports.

**DoD:**
- API/partner integrations create net-new license volume.
- Partner usage does not bypass trust, terms or audit logs.
- API reliability and permissioning are production-grade.
- Enterprise buyers can pass procurement/security review.

## Stage 5 — 100x category infrastructure: market-leader operating model

**Goal:** EchoVault becomes the default trust/provenance/licensing standard for commercial AI/hybrid music.

**Build:**
- Provenance graph.
- Public trust standard.
- Annual/quarterly trust report.
- Advanced risk intelligence.
- Enterprise-grade audit trails.
- Marketplace intelligence for supply planning.
- Partner ecosystem.
- Claims-support playbooks.
- Legal/compliance operations.
- Possible insurance/indemnity exploration if legally and economically feasible.

**DoD:**
- Buyers use EchoVault certificate as a default compliance artifact.
- Competitors copy trust/provenance language.
- Repeat purchase and expansion revenue are strong.
- Marketplace has defensible data loops.
- Brand is associated with commercial safety and quality.
- Unit economics improve with scale rather than degrading.

## What not to build too early

- Full consumer streaming app.
- Social/fan network.
- Open upload.
- Fully automated approval.
- Enterprise API before licensing loop works.
- Custom legal deals for every buyer.
- Proprietary AI music generator before trust-layer demand is proven.

## Kill or redesign triggers

Redesign if:
- Buyers do not care about certificate/provenance.
- Buyers prefer generating music themselves even when offered curated licensed tracks.
- Review cost makes unit economics impossible.
- Legal risk cannot be reduced to an acceptable level.
- Artists do not submit enough quality work.
- Disputes or claims are too frequent.
- The product only sells through founder-led manual persuasion.

Kill if:
- No clear buyer segment pays after repeated tests.
- The only demand is from artists wanting distribution, not buyers wanting licenses.
- The platform cannot make stronger trust claims than existing alternatives.
- Legal counsel determines the model's core promise is misleading or indefensible.
