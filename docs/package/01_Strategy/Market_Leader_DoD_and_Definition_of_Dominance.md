# Market-Leader DoD and Definition of Dominance

## Purpose

This document defines what "done" means when EchoVault is no longer just an MVP but the leading trusted licensing layer for AI/hybrid music.

Do not confuse these levels:

| Level | Meaning | Primary proof |
|---|---|---|
| MVP DoD | The first complete licensing loop works | Paid purchase, certificate, delivery, review flow |
| Product DoD | The product works repeatedly without founder hand-holding | Repeat buyers, predictable operations, stable quality |
| Market-Leader DoD | EchoVault is the default trust infrastructure for AI/hybrid music licensing | Category reputation, liquidity, integrations, legal trust, repeatable growth |

## Strategic category claim

EchoVault should not claim to be "Spotify for AI music."

The stronger category claim is:

> **The trusted licensing and provenance layer for commercially usable AI/hybrid music.**

## Definition of Dominance

EchoVault is approaching category leadership only when all eight dimensions are true.

### 1. Buyer trust dominance

**Done when:**
- Buyers understand allowed use before checkout without needing support.
- Every purchased track includes a clear, versioned license certificate.
- Buyers can share a public/private verification link with clients, platforms or auditors.
- Claim-support workflows are documented, trackable and responsive.
- Buyers associate EchoVault with lower licensing anxiety than generic AI generators or undifferentiated stock libraries.

**Evidence threshold:**
- Repeat buyer rate is material, not anecdotal.
- Support tickets show clarification requests decreasing over time.
- Buyers cite trust/provenance as a purchase reason in interviews and surveys.

**Evidence label:** Inferred.

### 2. Catalog quality dominance

**Done when:**
- Approved tracks are consistently usable in real commercial contexts.
- Catalog quality is curated enough that buyers do not need to sift through AI spam.
- The platform has reliable genre/use-case coverage for the first selected verticals.
- Rejection patterns improve artist submissions over time.
- Human reviewers and EchoScore produce consistent, explainable quality decisions.

**Evidence threshold:**
- Preview-to-license conversion improves by genre/use-case cohort.
- Approval rate and sales rate converge around known high-quality supply segments.
- Buyer search sessions commonly end in a save, shortlist or purchase.

**Evidence label:** Inferred.

### 3. Provenance and legal-process dominance

**Done when:**
- Every approved track has a structured provenance record: creator identity, AI tool/model used if known, production process, DAW/manual work, samples declaration and rights warranty.
- Terms are versioned and attached immutably to purchases.
- Disputes suspend future sales without breaking existing license history.
- The product never markets itself as "risk-free"; it markets itself as documented, reviewed and commercially safer.
- Legal counsel has reviewed artist warranty, buyer license, dispute policy, data policy and copyright/provenance wording.

**Evidence threshold:**
- Legal review completed.
- Dispute/audit process tested with mock cases.
- No paid license exists without terms version and provenance snapshot.

**Evidence label:** Observed for the need; Inferred for the operating model.

### 4. Marketplace liquidity dominance

**Done when:**
- Buyers can find commercially usable tracks quickly.
- Artists see enough licensing revenue or status benefit to continue submitting high-quality work.
- The platform avoids cold marketplace failure by focusing on clear demand verticals first.
- Supply growth does not lower trust.
- Demand informs what supply is recruited.

**Evidence threshold:**
- Paid licenses per approved track per month is stable or rising.
- Artist retention among approved contributors is positive.
- Top search queries map to available, licensable supply.

**Evidence label:** Inferred.

### 5. Workflow dominance

**Done when:**
- A creator/agency can go from need to usable file and certificate in minutes.
- Project workflows support shortlist, client approval, license upgrade, invoice, download history and certificate sharing.
- Agencies can manage multiple clients/projects without messy manual tracking.
- License records become part of the buyer's production/compliance workflow.

**Evidence threshold:**
- Time-to-licensed-file is consistently low.
- Repeat users create projects/collections rather than one-off downloads only.
- Buyer teams share certificates externally.

**Evidence label:** Inferred.

### 6. Platform/API dominance

**Done when:**
- EchoVault can be embedded into creator tools, video workflows, game/dev tools or agency systems.
- API users can search, preview, license and retrieve certificates under strict permissioning.
- API usage creates incremental demand rather than undercutting marketplace trust.
- Partner integrations produce measurable GMV or active buyer expansion.

**Evidence threshold:**
- API customers create paid license volume.
- Integration partners reduce CAC or increase retention.
- API delivery has productized terms and rate limits.

**Evidence label:** Speculative until partnerships and API demand are proven.

### 7. Data-learning dominance

**Done when:**
- Operational data improves catalog review, buyer matching, fraud detection, pricing, recommendations and artist guidance.
- EchoScore and PoE decisions are versioned, auditable and improved by feedback loops.
- The data loop is unique because it combines audio features, provenance, purchase behavior, claims/disputes and human review.

**Evidence threshold:**
- Review accuracy and buyer conversion improve with data.
- Recommendation quality measurably improves discovery.
- Fraud/risk rules reduce bad submissions without blocking good supply.

**Evidence label:** Speculative until enough data exists.

### 8. Brand/category dominance

**Done when:**
- EchoVault is referenced as a trusted standard for AI/hybrid music licensing.
- Buyers and artists understand what the brand stands for.
- The company can credibly publish quality/provenance standards.
- Competitors must respond to EchoVault's trust vocabulary, certificate format or provenance norms.

**Evidence threshold:**
- Inbound demand from buyers, artists and partners.
- Earned mentions, partner requests or industry references.
- Branded terms like EchoScore/PoE are understood by target users.

**Evidence label:** Speculative.

## Market-leader anti-fantasy checks

Do not claim market leadership because:
- The UI looks premium.
- The catalog is large but low-converting.
- The company has many artists but few buyers.
- EchoScore produces numbers that buyers do not trust.
- PoE exists but is not validated or reviewed.
- Licenses exist but legal terms are weak.
- The product has traffic but no repeat commercial usage.
- The platform has an API but no embedded workflow demand.

## Final dominance test

EchoVault is not the market leader when people listen to AI music there.

EchoVault becomes the market leader when commercial buyers say:

> "If this music is going into client work, monetized content, a game, an ad or a public campaign, we need the EchoVault certificate/provenance record."
