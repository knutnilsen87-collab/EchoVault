# Current Market Context Source Notes

## Purpose

These notes inform the strategic and legal risk framing. They are not legal advice.

## Source notes

1. Suno help center states that songs made while subscribed to Pro/Premier plans are granted commercial-use rights, and related help pages state paid subscribers are considered owners of songs created while subscribed. This makes "generate it yourself" a serious substitute for EchoVault in some buyer segments.

2. Mubert publicly positions itself around royalty-free AI-generated music for content and offers an AI Music API for apps, games, agents and content platforms. This overlaps with EchoVault's potential marketplace/API expansion.

3. RIAA and major record companies have brought copyright cases against Suno and Udio. This confirms that AI music copyright/training-data risk remains a major market concern and supports the need for a careful trust/provenance posture.

4. The European Commission's AI Act materials describe GPAI obligations including technical documentation, copyright policy and summaries of training content for GPAI model providers. EchoVault is not necessarily a GPAI model provider if it only uses third-party tools and operates a marketplace, but the broader regulatory direction reinforces the need for documentation, transparency and process control.

## Strategic implication

EchoVault cannot win by being "AI music exists here." Competitors can generate music. EchoVault must win by being the trusted commercial-use workflow and provenance layer.
